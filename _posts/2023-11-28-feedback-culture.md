Why should you bother what other people think about you? That's because, other people can promote you, improve yourself or help you in the future. Feedback can really change how you work or how you do things in life and that's a good thing. But when feedback comes without context, when it comes as gossip, that's not professional nor it should matter to you. I'll explain.

Think like a memory, a feedback is a memory that you and other person shared. Good or bad, it is yours. But there is good news: that memory is like a impression that someone caused, and that can be changed and improved over time. That's why you need to keep track of it. You need to know regularly if that impression, that memory has changed to a better one.

Let me clear things out: I`m not saying that a person can forget if you were rude to her. I'm saying that if you show, with your actions, that you is improving your maners, your comunication or your technical skills, them there is a chance that a bad feedback can be let in the past.

Feedbacks reflect who were you in some point in time. If you are not that person anymore, so that feedback can rest in peace.

## Asking for feedback

I have a habit to ask for feedback from all my peers and leadership every 1 or 2 months. This give me knowledge on how I'm doing: if I'm doing things the way that is expected, if my coleagues find it nice to work with me, If I`m not desrespecting someone and etc...

The feedback sessions generally start by myself explaing why I requested the meeting and If the person is confortable to give me some feedback, positive or negative about my work or how I relate to them. I also ask if I can give feedback.

I`ve being doing that for the past 5 years and that habit has given me many insights on how to improve myself, how to help my team and how to make my way to promotion and being notice by the managers. Some people that I ask for feedback initially are not confortable with that. But, after some sessions, they will start to talk more. 

I prefer to write what I'll say to each person, good or bad. This gives something very important to feedback: `context`. I can not say just "you did that", that's not a good feedback, is too vague and very open do interpretation which leads to bad comunication, very unconfortable talks. So, I prefer to give the full picture.

What I'm trying to show with this article is that feedback should be given some thought process and not just be given. Sometimes people pass through stressfull situations and they can be rude sometimes, but if the person is not rude all the time the bigger problem was the `stressfull situation`. Giving the full context will enable you to find the real problem and this will improve the relatioship with your colleagues.

## Feedback faces

No one expect that you will ask for feedback every two weeks or so. Problably your company has a method to evalute the performance of its employees and your manager will be giving the feedback to you. And the your colleagues? What they think about you? They work with you every day, even when your manager is not around. This makes them a valuable source for feedback.

Of course inside a company there is also some way a colleague can give a feedback to you, but, generally speaking, this type of feedback is sometimes anonymous and can be too simple. Like a grade, stars or setences that show how your pair fit in the company culture. That's not what someone who wants personal feedback needs, that is what the company/manager needs to identify possible ways to improve the team.

Every team member should ask all pairs for feedback, and here I'm saying that a pair is somenone that you worked closely with in the feedback period. By doing that you will get a feeling how the team sees you and how you can improve. If you notice some pattern in the feedbacks, for example, 3 team members said that you should improve your SOLID skills. With that information you can now take action and study SOLID, or other patterns to make your code more simple or clear. Feedbacks are valuable resources to drive your carrer, they enable you to be more present in your team and to now how things are going. It's a great way to build `trust`.

Doing this feedback sessions, you will notice a change: team members will gradually trust more in you and will talk more too. With this you will gain access to what they thing about the team, the way in what things are done and more. This a valuable asset. If you is a staff engineer or a principal, what can you do with that information? Maybe start to try to change things. And if you are not in those positions: as your knowledge and experience grows, you can become a reference to them.

Just remember: in a feedback session you should listen more than talk. If a person says something to you that make you angry, take a deep breath and answer in a polite way explaning the missunderstood and remember to think "is this person right about this?". If the answer is "yes" to the question, then you should take another deep breath and continue to undstand yourself, which is the hardest part. There is no problem to point that a bad feedback actually was a missunderstanding or just was`nt you that did what the person is talking about. There is no problem eather in point out that you need more context in a feedback and start makind questions to your pair: when did that happen? In which meeting? How was the project in that period? How you felt? Just becarful to not enter in a defensive possition or a anger position. This is not ok and will drive away your colleague.

As a manager you should not only give feedback, but also asks for feedback all the time. Receiving feedback as a leader is a good way to keep a nice relationship to to your team and again build trust. This could make a difference to make the team healthier. If you receive well a bad feedback you gain not only a chance to improve, but a chance to demonstrate that you are a solid leader, a leader that listens and is there for the team. 

Investigate feedbacks is also something that a leader must do.  For example, if somene says that Joey was rude to Ross. Your job should be investigating why that has happen and how you can prevent from happening again. You can do that talking with the people involved in the issue, but also understanding the moment of the team and talking to others team members not directly involved in the incident. Before make judgement of what happend, try to ask: Is there some kind of pressure to deliver a feature? Was someone fired? Is that fired person related with Joey? Is there some kind of stress situation goind on? Ross said something or did something that irritated Joey?

Those questions should give a nice view on why Joey was rude to Ross. With that view you can provide the correct tone of the conversation. Joey, should not be reprimanded if it was just one case, one time because of stress. He should be given some direction to correct that behavior and learn how to deal with stress. On the other hand, if Joey was rude because he is not polite, because a child reason or even if it`s not the first time, then he need a more hard talk to not do that again and put him under observation for a period. Putting people in observation is a nice way to control your feedback system, who is doing fine and who doesn't.

And not only managers can put people under observation. Ross could put Joey in observation: if was the first time that this has happend, Ross could wait for this to happen another time and only them talk to Joey about it or if it is a serious case, talk to a manager.

## Feedback Period

A part of the feedback context is the period of the feedback. If we are in 2023 and someone is talking to you of a thing that happened in 2013, there is something wrong, don`t you think?

Generally stay in the middle of this: not too long period for people to forget things and not too short for people find the feedback sessions anoying. In my case I try to do feedback sessions every 1 and half month to 2 months. It dependes on how the project is doing, the stress level of the team and the time that we have. This feedback period works for me, in my team, in the company that I work and in my current project. Try to find the period that works best for you given your context.

Context is the most important part of the feedback and its the sum of the period + some event + people involed.

## Feedback context

Feedback withou a context is not feedback, is gossip!

Now that we have a good understanding of how feedback works, let's talk about the most important part of the feedback: `context`. Whenever you ask or receive a feedback, it must has a context. But what I mean about that? Feedback has to have a period for refence, it needs some story associate with it for you know in which circustances the feedback occurred.

Let`s compare feedback with agile methodology Scrum. In scrum you have a sprint which is a period to deliver somethings, a sprint can 2 two to 4 weeks long, after that period you have the review and the retrospective. Both meetings are feedback meetings where the team can address things that were bad and continue doing things that worked. Of course, there are differences between review (more business focused) and retrospective (more team focused), but for simplicity I will not enter in that. Basically, If you have worked with any agile or prototype methodology you would always have some kind of loop which is as follows: 

<<<<<<<<<<<<<<<<SPRINT>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>
Plan -> Act -> Review -> Retrospective
^                           |
|                           |
|                           |
|___________________________|

If we look at this process what is the context? We plan, so there is a project that is part of the context, there is a period of two weeks, there is what was develivered in the period and finally there is what happens during the period. When giving feedback is very important to bring context to the conversation try to always remember when the event happend, how it happend and which moment the team was when the event happend. In this way you can give the best feedback possible, because you give all information needed for people to improve themselves.

Let`s use a example to make things more clear:

Joey and Ross are team members. One day Ross was reviewing a Pull Request from Joey and wrote the comment:

"This unit test sucks! You should rewrite it"

This message is not telling anything at all. It`s just a curse, nothing else. A more beautiful and meaning message would be: 

"Your unit test is not testing the class (unit), but the entire system. Also it violates our testing guidelines. Use this link as reference https://mytestdoc.company.com"

This is a much better message, because context was given. The person that reading it will know exactaly where it needs to improve. 

Another example:

Joey, you was very rude with me during the planning last week, that's not cool.

This is not a nice way to give feedback. Because it's another empty message, there is no context all all. So how a person can improve if her does'nt where to start? A better message could be:

Joey, in the planning last week, when we were discussing about using a Realm or Core Data you was rude with me. I was given my sugestion and you said that my idea was just pointless, without even listening to the full context and the full idea. You abruptally interrupted me. I`m not ok with that... Next time, wait for the full idea to give your opinion about it and be more polite with it. I think you would not be ok with me saying that your idea is pointless.

And one more example:

Ross, all the time you escalate our dicussions to our manager. 

This type of feedback is a exaggeration of something that happend. There is no problem with the exaggeration itself, people tend to do that when they are upset. But this sentence brings up some questions: when did that happen? Is it really all the time?

A better feedback message would be: 

Ross, every time we have a dicussion you bring that to our manager. You could have talk to me directly instead and we would solve the matter between us.

This is a better feedback message, now we have a full context. The period, the story, the people involved and moment of the project. With this message the person that receives it can search for a way to improve and be better.

Give or receive, feedback is a difficult thing. You have to listen more than you talk, you have to know the full picture, you have to stay calm during the process and be empathetic. Just remember that feedback is a good thing, is a present that someone is giving: `a chance to better`. But feedback without context has little value, so always pay attention to the context. Feedback is a continuos process, like any agile methodology is a process of contignous improvement, this means that you need to do it many times until you find your on way.
